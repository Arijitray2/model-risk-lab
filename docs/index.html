$whoami --project

A market-making teaching idea, rebuilt as a statistically rigorous model-risk study.

A market maker earns the bid–ask spread by quoting around fair value — but fair value comes from a model, and the model can be wrong. This project makes that danger measurable end-to-end:

[01] CALIBRATE

Fit the models to real markets

Maximum-likelihood estimation of GBM and Merton jump-diffusion on SPY and NIFTY 50 daily returns — standard errors, likelihood-ratio tests, AIC/BIC, QQ diagnostics. Risk-neutral Merton calibration to four real SPY option-chain snapshots, including 16 March 2020 (VIX 82): the COVID options market priced λ = 3.7 crashes/year with mean jump −31%.

[02] SIMULATE

Run the desk under model risk

A rolling constant-maturity options desk quotes bid/ask around its believed fair value; every fill's delta-hedged life is realised under the true dynamics. Client flow is price-elastic — a mispriced quote is punished by adverse selection, not ignored. Four scenarios: matched, steamroller, right-model, overcautious.

[03] MEASURE

Price the model risk

Across hundreds of seeds: VaR and Expected Shortfall of desk P/L, drawdown distributions, and a model-risk premium — the extra spread a desk must charge to survive its own model uncertainty, found by break-even search.

[04] VALIDATE

Catch the broken model

Kupiec and Christoffersen VaR backtests (the mispriced desk blows through its 5% VaR on 54% of days, p < 10−16), bootstrap confidence intervals on realised edge, and a CUSUM monitor that raises the alarm at trade 83 of 1,467 — months before the equity curve makes it obvious.

$cat design-principles

  1. Real data, not assumed parameters. Every model parameter in the headline experiments is estimated from market data — physical measure from return histories, risk-neutral measure from option chains — with uncertainty quantified.
  2. The estimator is honest. Where inference is delicate (a likelihood-ratio test with a boundary null, discrete hedging error, an incomplete market under jumps) the write-up says so rather than hiding it.
  3. Validation is the product. The point is not that a wrong model loses money — it's that a validator armed with coverage tests and sequential monitors can detect the wrong model from realised P/L, early, with controlled false-alarm rates.
